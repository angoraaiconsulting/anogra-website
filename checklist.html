The Curse
of the Curve

Everyone has felt this. AI is magic for a single task and a mess for a real workflow.

One great answer, then it falls apart the next day. One person figures it out, then they go on vacation. The tool saves an hour on Tuesday and creates two hours of cleanup on Wednesday.

That gap has a name. This checklist tells you which side of it you are on.

01Repeatability

Your best AI result could not be reproduced next week.

Someone got a great output once. Nobody saved the prompt, nobody wrote the steps, and the magic never happened again.

Fix: Save what worked. A result you cannot reproduce is luck, not a system.

02Capture

If the person who built it left, the workflow dies with them.

The good prompts live in one person's head. When they are out sick or move on, the team starts from zero.

Fix: Write it down where the team can find it. If it is not written, it does not exist.

03Verification

AI output reaches real decisions without a human checking it.

Schedules, billing, crew assignments. The output looks right, so it goes out. Nobody confirmed it.

Fix: Decide who reviews the output and when, before it counts.

04Sharing

Every department reinvents what another already solved.

Dispatch prompts one way, billing another, HR a third. Nobody knows what is working two desks over.

Fix: Collect the three best prompts in the company and make them everyone's starting point.

05Ownership

When a workflow breaks, fixing it is nobody's job.

It worked in March. Something changed in June. Nobody noticed until the errors piled up.

Fix: Name an owner. Someone's job is to notice and fix it.

06Boundaries

You have not written down what AI must never touch.

So the team either avoids AI entirely or uses it where they should not. Both are expensive.

Fix: Write the do-not-automate list first. Dispatch decisions, the patient care record, clinical judgment. The boundary is what makes the rest safe.

07Tool discipline

You are paying for AI tools with no workflow assigned to them.

Licenses renew every month. Nobody can point to the workflow each tool runs.

Fix: Match every tool to one real workflow, or cancel it. The tool is the last decision, not the first.
